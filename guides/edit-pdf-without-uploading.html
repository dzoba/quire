How to edit a PDF without uploading it

Short answer: use a tool that runs on your computer rather than a website. The PDF viewers in Chrome and Edge and Preview on a Mac fill forms, add text and sign without uploading anything. For page changes, free desktop apps such as PDFsam Basic work offline, and Chrome extensions such as Quire PDF process files in the browser.

Online PDF sites work by uploading your file. Most say they delete it within an hour or two, but it has still left your computer.

Updated 15 October 2026. Retention periods are quoted from each service’s own pages, checked 14 October 2026; they can change.

Edit a PDF locally, step by step

  1. Save the PDF to your computer

    Download the file instead of opening it in an online editor. A PDF in your Downloads folder can be opened by any of the tools below.

  2. Open it in a local tool

    Drag it into a Chrome window, double-click it to open it in Edge on Windows or Preview on a Mac, or open it in a desktop app or a local Chrome extension.

  3. Make your changes

    Fill in fields, add text, highlight or sign. These all happen on your computer.

  4. Save to your own disk

    Download or save the edited copy to a folder on your computer. Avoid Save to Drive or other cloud options if you want the file to stay local.

  5. Check before sharing

    Open the saved copy once. Anything you send by email or upload from here on is your choice, not the editor’s.

What runs on your computer

  • Chrome’s PDF viewer: highlight, draw, add text, fill forms and sign, then Download › With your changes. Google says even its OCR for scanned PDFs happens on the device. Its Save to Drive option uploads to your Google Drive, so skip it if you want the file local. See how to annotate a PDF in Chrome.
  • Preview on a Mac: fill and sign forms, add text, highlights and notes, and combine, reorder or delete pages.
  • Microsoft Edge on Windows: fill forms, add text and notes, highlight, and save a copy. See how to edit a PDF on Windows without Acrobat.
  • A Chromebook’s Gallery app: text, highlights, signatures, and rotating and reordering pages. See how to edit a PDF on a Chromebook.
  • PDFsam Basic (Windows, Mac, Linux): merge, split, extract and rotate. Its makers say it “runs entirely on your own computer, so nothing gets uploaded and no internet connection is needed.”
  • PDF24 Creator (Windows): page tools and compression. Its makers say files stay on your PC. PDF24’s website tools are a different thing and do upload.

Quire PDF: local editing in Chrome

Quire PDF is our Chrome extension, and it was built to work locally. Documents are processed on your computer. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it doesn’t send your PDFs to us or to any processing service.

  • It adds highlights, notes, text, drawings and images, fills forms, places a reusable signature, and reorders, removes, combines and exports pages.
  • Your library, recovery copies and saved signatures stay in your Chrome profile on this computer. They aren’t synced or backed up, so keep the copy you download.
  • To open a PDF from a link, Quire asks for access to that one website and fetches the file from it directly, without sending that site’s cookies. If the site needs a sign-in, download the PDF and open the file instead.
  • It has no OCR, secure redaction or certificate-based signing, and it adds to the page rather than rewriting existing text.

What online editors say they do with your file

From their own pages: iLovePDF says processed files are permanently deleted within two hours, though it keeps signed documents for up to five years for legal reasons. Smallpdf says files from its free tools are deleted within an hour, and files you store in an account stay until you delete them. PDF24 says files are deleted from its processing servers within an hour.

Those are reasonable policies, and for a flyer or a menu they’re fine. For tax forms, medical letters, contracts or anything with an ID number, a local tool means no copy ever reaches someone else’s server.

Questions

Do Chrome extensions upload PDFs?

Some do: many PDF extensions are front ends to an online service. Read the listing and privacy policy. Quire PDF’s privacy policy explains what it stores and where.

Can I edit a PDF with no internet connection at all?

Once the PDF is on your computer, the desktop tools above don’t need a connection; PDFsam’s makers say so explicitly. Chrome’s viewer and Quire PDF open local files too.
